# Roadmap

Sequenced against [docs/Vision.md](docs/Vision.md). Each phase is scoped to ship something usable and to avoid re-architecting later phases — see "Foundations this phase requires" under each entry.

## Phase 0 — Mortgage Engine (done)

Loan splits (fixed/floating), automatic refixing, offset accounts with refix top-up transfers, revolving credit, recurring extra/lump-sum payments, daily-equivalent amortisation schedule per split, portfolio-level totals.

**Gaps against Vision §5** (Mortgage Engine) found on review, not yet covered above:
- Interest is periodic-equivalent (`annualRate / periodsPerYear` applied per payment), not true day-count accrual — Vision §5 specifies "Interest calculated daily," and this is core simulation math, not a UI nicety
- No Interest-Only period or Repayment Type field on `MortgageSplit`/`MortgageProduct`
- No refinance event (only `RateChangeEvent`/`BalanceAdjustmentEvent` exist)
- No "Effective Interest Rate" output on `MortgageResult`
- Offset balance is static for the life of the loan — no accrual, no ongoing deposits modelled; needs an explicit decision on whether that's intended v1 scope

**Do now, before Phase 1** — either quick cleanup or touches the simulation core that Phase 1's `Liability` abstraction will be built around, so worth getting right once rather than migrating broken math twice:
- Fix known bugs: duplicate-split naming ([MortgageCalculator.tsx](src/components/mortgage/MortgageCalculator.tsx)), offset top-up transfer loop bailing early ([SimulationRunner.ts](src/engines/SimulationRunner.ts))
- Enable `strict` TypeScript
- Remove dead scaffold classes (`Mortgage.ts`, `LoanSplit.ts`, `InterestRate.ts`, empty stubs) superseded by the interface-based domain model
- Make `SimulationRunner`'s recurring-payment/transaction lookups O(n) instead of O(n²) — this becomes load-bearing once Monte Carlo (Phase 6) runs thousands of simulations
- Switch interest accrual to daily/day-count
- Memoise the `MortgageEngine.simulate()` call in the UI so typing doesn't re-run the full amortisation on every keystroke

**Defer to just after Phase 1** — additive fields that would just get reshaped again once the generic `Liability` type lands, so build them on that shape directly instead of on `MortgageSplit` twice:
- Interest-Only period, Repayment Type field
- Refinance event, effective-rate output

**Post-Phase-4 engine enhancement** — general refix redistribution, requested once Phases 1–4 made "compare repayment strategies" a concrete goal:
- `RefixBalanceTarget` (`src/domain/mortgage/RefixBalanceTarget.ts`) lets *any* split opt into a value, percentage-of-mortgage, or weighted-share-of-remainder target; `SimulationRunner` resolves every participating split's target at each refix and nets out whatever transfers (either direction) are needed to hit them — a real rebalance, not the old one-directional "drain sources to top up one offset split." Replaces `OffsetSettings.maintainLoanBalanceAtRefix`/`refixLoanBalanceTarget`.
- Found and fixed along the way: the day-count interest engine (Phase 0) didn't exactly match the periodic-rate annuity formula sizing the level repayment, so a fully-amortising loan could finish its scheduled term with a small non-zero residual (e.g. $270 on a $100k/30yr loan) instead of $0 — invisible until a new "loan completion date" stat made it visible. The final scheduled payment now truncates to clear the balance exactly, like a real payoff statement.
- `MortgageSummary.payoffDate` — the mortgage's overall completion date, shown on `SummaryCard`; undefined (shown as "—") unless every split actually reaches zero (e.g. absent if a revolving-credit split is present).
- **Extra payment advisor** (`src/domain/mortgage/recommendExtraPayment.ts`, shown on `MortgagePanel`): answers "which split should extra money go to?" by literally re-simulating each candidate split with a test lump sum applied and comparing total interest against a baseline — not a nominal-rate heuristic, which would wrongly favour a fully-offset split's high paper rate over its near-zero effective cost. Confirmed by test: a fully-offset split at 8% correctly shows $0 saved against a plain 5% P&I split showing real savings. Stops short of a full multi-split optimizer (search across ways to divide the amount) — that stays Phase 5/6 territory.

## Phase 1 — Domain Foundations (done)

Generalise the mortgage-only domain into the shape everything else builds on. This is the highest-leverage phase — get it wrong and later phases force rework.

- **Money/currency**: `Money` (`src/domain/money/Money.ts`) now carries a currency (default NZD) through its factories and arithmetic, and throws on cross-currency `add`/`subtract`. Day-count itself landed in Phase 0.
- **Ownership model**: `OwnershipEntity` (`src/domain/shared/OwnershipEntity.ts`, Individual/Joint/Trust/Company/LTC/Partnership) and `OwnershipStake` (`src/domain/shared/Ownership.ts`), reused by both Assets and Liabilities
- **Asset abstraction**: `AssetBase`/`Asset` union (`src/domain/asset/Asset.ts`) with one concrete `OtherAsset` member proving the pattern — Property/Cash/Shares/etc. join as Phase 2/3 design their own fields
- **Liability abstraction**: `LiabilityBase`/`Liability` union (`src/domain/liability/Liability.ts`) with `MortgageLiability` (`src/domain/mortgage/MortgageLiability.ts`) as the concrete type — introduces the "unlimited mortgages, each with unlimited splits" grouping the Vision calls for, wrapping the existing `MortgageSplit[]` untouched
- **Portfolio aggregate**: `Portfolio` (`src/domain/portfolio/Portfolio.ts`) — entities/assets/liabilities, plain serializable data, no methods
- **Persistence**: `src/services/PortfolioStorage.ts` (versioned `{schemaVersion, portfolio}` localStorage read/write) behind `src/store/PortfolioContext.tsx` + `usePortfolio()` — the only two functions that know about `localStorage`, so a future SQLite move is a storage-adapter swap, not a rewrite

`MortgageCalculator` now reads/writes through `usePortfolio()` instead of local `useState`, via a `toMortgageInput` adapter — `SimulationRunner`/`MortgageEngine` needed zero changes. Verified: build/lint clean, no visible regression in the UI, and edits now survive a page reload.

## Phase 3 — Cash Flow & Savings (done)

- **`CashAccount`** (`src/domain/cash/CashAccount.ts`) joins the `Asset` union — everyday/savings/term-deposit, reusing `AssetBase.currentValue` as the balance. `AssetType`'s Phase-1 placeholder `"cash"` was refined into `"everyday" | "savings"` now that this module actually designed the shape.
- **Offset accounts link to a real `CashAccount`**: `OffsetSettings.linkedAccountId?` resolved to `offsetBalance` one layer up in `toMortgageInput` (now accepting the portfolio's assets) — `SimulationRunner` itself needed **zero changes**. Verified the resolved balance produces identical interest figures to a manually-entered offset balance.
- **`calculateCashFlow`** (`src/domain/cashflow/calculateCashFlow.ts`) annualises property income/expenses and real mortgage repayments (via the engine, not a duplicated manual entry) onto one figure, then derives weekly/fortnightly/monthly/annual rows — rendered by `CashFlowSummaryCard` on the home screen.
- **Deferred**: real balance simulation (cash accounts stay manually-tracked, like property `currentValue`) and "automatic transfers" (nets to zero on a consolidated report; meaningful modelling needs the balance-projection work Phase 5/6 owns) — both explicitly scoped out with the user up front, not silently dropped.
- **Reversed after shipping**: this phase originally also added portfolio-level `CashFlowItem[]` (salary, dividends, outside interest, other income; insurance, living costs, tax, other expenses — items not tied to a property). The user later decided to keep the whole project scoped to property/investment business rather than personal finances, so this was removed entirely — `Portfolio.cashFlowItems`, the domain type, the "Income & Expenses" UI section, and its contribution to `calculateCashFlow`/`passiveIncomeAnnual`. The consolidated cash flow position (and the Dashboard's Passive Income stat) is property + mortgage only, by design, going forward.

Verified: build/lint clean, math hand-checked against the UI (income/expense/net totals matched exactly), the offset-linking regression-tested against Phase 0's manual offset figures, and everything survives a reload — including on genuinely old pre-Phase-3 `localStorage` data.

## Phase 4 — Dashboard (done)

- **`calculateDashboardStats`** (`src/domain/dashboard/calculateDashboardStats.ts`) computes all 15 non-optional stats (net worth, total assets/liabilities/mortgage debt/offset/cash/equity, LVR, weekly/monthly/annual cash flow via `calculateCashFlow`, total interest paid, forecast interest remaining, portfolio return, passive income) from a `Portfolio` plus a real wall-clock "as of" date — the first place in the app that distinction matters, splitting each mortgage's amortisation schedule into paid-so-far vs. still-ahead.
- **`calculateDashboardCharts`** (`src/domain/dashboard/calculateDashboardCharts.ts`) supplies the four charts computable from data that exists today — Asset Allocation, Property Values, Loan Balance and cumulative Interest Paid over time (the latter two resampled onto a shared calendar-year grid across every mortgage split, since schedules differ in frequency/start/length). Net Worth / Debt Reduction / Equity Growth over time are deferred — genuine multi-year trend charts need forward growth simulation across all assets, which is Phase 5/6's job.
- **FIRE Progress skipped** (Vision marks it optional; needs a withdrawal-rate assumption nothing else in the app asks for). Portfolio Return is defined as gross rental yield, labelled as such rather than implying true time-weighted investment return.
- Chart components (`src/components/dashboard/`) built per the `dataviz` skill: inline SVG, the skill's validated reference categorical/sequential palette (fixed asset-type → color mapping so a category never repaints), hover tooltips + crosshair on the trend lines, always-visible legends (no value gated behind hover).
- **Dashboard is now the home screen** (`src/App.tsx` gained a `view` state + simple nav); Properties (property list, cash accounts, income/expenses — all Phase 2/3 work, unchanged) sits one click away.

Verified: build/lint clean; every stat hand-reconciled exactly against known portfolio state (including the paid+remaining-interest identity check landing on Phase 0/3's already-verified offset figures); charts checked with real data and with an empty portfolio (no NaN/Infinity — LVR and Portfolio Return guard the zero-property case).

## Phase 5 — Strategy Simulator & Scenario Manager (done)

The first feature that projects the *whole portfolio* forward through calendar time rather than recording current state or simulating one mortgage over its own term. `AssetBase.annualGrowthRate` and `CashAccount.interestRate` — unused since Phase 1/3 — finally get a purpose here.

- **`projectPortfolio`** (`src/domain/scenario/projectPortfolio.ts`): year-by-year projection built almost entirely on existing, already-verified primitives rather than new simulation logic. Each mortgage (real or scenario-originated) is still simulated in full by the unchanged `SimulationRunner`/`MortgageEngine`, then resampled onto a calendar-year grid via `annualSeriesForSchedule`/`valueAtYear` — extracted from `calculateDashboardCharts.ts` into `src/domain/mortgage/annualSeries.ts` so the Dashboard charts and the scenario engine share one implementation instead of two. Properties grow by their own `annualGrowthRate` (falling back to the scenario's `propertyGrowthRate`); rent/expenses grow by the scenario's `rentalGrowthRate`/`expenseInflationRate`; cash compounds by each account's own `interestRate`, with the year's net cash flow surplus/deficit and any one-off event cash (deposits, sale proceeds, renovation cost) flowing into a single designated account.
- **Strategy events** (`src/domain/scenario/StrategyEvent.ts`): a five-member discriminated union — `buy-property`, `sell-property`, `renovate`, `increase-repayments`, `refinance` — each firing on Jan 1 of its "years from today" offset, keeping the whole projection at clean annual resolution instead of drifting off today's day-of-year (this matters: an origination mid-calendar-year would credit a full year of new rent against a partial year of loan interest, and vice-versa for the deficit). `refinance` reuses the existing-but-previously-unused `RateChangeEvent`; `increase-repayments` reuses the existing `RecurringPayment` mechanism directly — no new engine primitives were needed for either. A `buy-property` event's own `id` deterministically derives its synthetic property/split ids, so a later event in the same scenario (renovate/sell/refinance/increase against a property bought earlier in the same timeline) can target it.
- **`Scenario`** (`src/domain/scenario/Scenario.ts`, `createDefaultScenario.ts`): `{ id, name, assumptions, events }`, persisted on `Portfolio.scenarios` (new array field, defaulted via `?? []` in `PortfolioStorage` for portfolios saved before this field existed — same pattern as every prior additive field).
- UI: `ScenarioList` (create/select/delete, mounted as a new "Strategies" nav item) → `ScenarioDetail` (assumptions form + event timeline, one row per type via `StrategyEventRow`, property/split pickers listing both real portfolio entities and any earlier buy-event's synthetic ones) → `ScenarioComparison` (Baseline — today's portfolio, default assumptions, no events — vs. the scenario: a stat table plus a genuine multi-series `MultiSeriesTrendChart` for Net Worth, built fresh rather than stretching Phase 4's single-series `TrendLineChart`; palette validated via the dataviz skill's script before shipping).
- **Scope, confirmed with the user before implementation**: strategy actions stay property/mortgage-only — Vision's "Invest elsewhere" and "Debt recycle" both mean releasing equity into non-property assets, directly the territory removed from the app in the cash-flow simplification earlier this phase, so both are excluded. Scenarios are persisted, not a throwaway calculator.
- **Deferred**: **Build Offset** and **Split Mortgage** (Vision's remaining two strategy actions — need machinery this pass doesn't build: user-directed surplus routing, and a "replace one split with several" operation); **N-way scenario comparison** (this pass is Baseline-vs-one-scenario; comparing several saved scenarios is a UI extension on the same engine); **cash-flow/debt trend charts in the comparison** (only Net Worth is charted this pass, cash flow and debt stay in the stat table); **Monte Carlo** stays Phase 6 — this phase's engine is exactly what Phase 6 will call thousands of times with randomised assumptions, so its shape mattered more than its randomisation.

Verified: `tsc`/`eslint` clean. Engine-level (dynamic import): a `buy-property` event correctly excludes the origination-year principal draw from that year's cash flow (a real bug caught and fixed — the naive diff-against-prior-year formula double-counted the new loan's principal as a cash inflow); a `sell-property` event correctly zeroes future rent/expenses/interest-accrual and adds net sale proceeds in the sale year; the Baseline projection is stable and never mutates the source portfolio. Browser: created a scenario, added `buy-property` then `sell-property` events, confirmed the comparison table and chart (with legend) updated correctly and consistently at each step, confirmed the property/split pickers correctly listed a synthetic buy-event property alongside real ones, confirmed scenarios and their events persist across a full reload, console clean throughout.

## Phase 6 — Monte Carlo Simulation (done)

Runs the unchanged Phase 5 `projectPortfolio` many times against randomised copies of a scenario's assumptions, rather than building a second simulation engine. Two things Vision left undefined were confirmed with the user before design: **"success"** is a user-set target net worth for the scenario's final projection year (not an insolvency check or a withdrawal-rate/FIRE metric — the latter was deliberately avoided, same reasoning as Phase 4 skipping FIRE Progress); **run count** is capped at **100/500/1,000**, executed synchronously on the main thread (no Web Worker infrastructure — this app has none yet, and a 1,000-run timing check below shows it isn't needed for this cap).

- **`runMonteCarloSimulation`** (`src/domain/montecarlo/runMonteCarloSimulation.ts`): for each run, builds a randomised copy of `scenario.assumptions` (via `sampleNormal` — Box-Muller, `src/domain/montecarlo/randomSampling.ts`) and calls `projectPortfolio` unchanged — zero changes to the Phase 5 engine. Strategy events (buy/sell/renovate/refinance/increase-repayments) stay fixed every run; only the continuous variables randomise. Each run draws **one value per variable, held for the whole projection** — not re-randomised year to year — a standard, deliberate simplification over a full stochastic walk. Interest rates only randomise when the scenario has `mortgageRateOverride` set (Phase 5's existing flat-rate mechanism); otherwise every run keeps each split's own configured/fixed rate, same as today.
- **Vacancy**, one of Vision's seven randomised variables, didn't exist as a concept anywhere — added as `ScenarioAssumptions.vacancyRate?: number` (`src/domain/scenario/Scenario.ts`), applied to `projectPortfolio`'s existing rent calculation rather than a separate sub-model. This also makes vacancy available in the regular (non-Monte-Carlo) scenario assumptions form and comparison — confirmed in the browser that changing it shifts the deterministic comparison, and a subsequent Monte Carlo re-run picks up the new baseline correctly. **Maintenance** (also in Vision's list) isn't its own forecast variable in the data model — folded into `expenseInflationRate`/its volatility rather than inventing a second, redundant variable.
- **Outputs**: probability of success, best/median/worst case, and risk metrics = standard deviation + P10/P90 ("risk band") of final-year net worth across all runs — all computed from the same run set, no new assumptions invented beyond what Vision asked for.
- **`NetWorthFanChart`** (`src/components/montecarlo/NetWorthFanChart.tsx`): P10/median/P90 net worth over time. Per the dataviz skill (reloaded for this phase): a percentile band is *one entity's uncertainty range*, not three identities, so it's a single-hue shaded band + solid median line — not three categorical lines, which is what the original plan sketched before the skill's own color-formula rule ("color follows the entity") argued for the band instead. Noted here as a deliberate refinement over the approved plan text, not a silent deviation.
- **`MonteCarloPanel`** (`src/components/montecarlo/MonteCarloPanel.tsx`, mounted in `ScenarioDetail` below `ScenarioComparison`): run-count/target/volatility config form, an explicit "Run simulation" button (not live-recompute — matches the Phase 0 memoisation discipline) deferred one tick via `setTimeout` so the loading state actually paints before the synchronous computation blocks the tab, then the result stats + fan chart.

Verified: `tsc`/`eslint` clean. Engine-level (dynamic import): `sampleNormal` over 20,000 samples matched its target mean/stddev closely (5.03/1.99 vs. inputs 5/2) and clamping held; `runMonteCarloSimulation` never mutated the source portfolio or scenario; a zero-volatility config made every run identical and collapsed exactly to `projectPortfolio`'s own deterministic result (936,923.30 both ways) — the randomised path degrades cleanly to the already-verified deterministic one. **1,000 runs against a two-split portfolio (one with fixed-rate refixing) took ~1.9 seconds** — acceptable for a one-off click with a loading state, confirming the synchronous/no-worker call was right for this run-count cap. Browser: ran Monte Carlo on a scenario, confirmed probability/best/worst/median/risk-band and the fan chart rendered correctly; changed vacancy rate and confirmed it shifted the deterministic comparison, then confirmed a re-run picked up the new numbers; console clean throughout.

## Phase 7 — Reporting & Export (done)

Vision's six report types, all built on one shared shape instead of six bespoke formats — confirmed with the user before design: **export formats** are CSV + JSON (built natively — this app has added zero dependencies in 6 phases) + PDF via the browser's native print dialog against a print stylesheet (`window.print()`, no library); **Excel deferred** (CSV already opens directly in Excel, so a real `.xlsx` writer was the lowest-value format to add a dependency for). **Navigation** is a dedicated "Reports" nav tab, matching how Vision frames Reporting as its own section.

- **`ReportData`** (`src/domain/reporting/ReportData.ts`): `{ title, generatedAt, sections: { heading, rows: { label, value }[] }[] }` — every report shares this shape, with row values pre-formatted strings, so CSV/JSON export is one generic serializer instead of six.
- **Six report builders** (`src/domain/reporting/build*Report.ts`) — no new domain logic, each just reshapes an already-verified computation: `buildPortfolioSummaryReport`/`buildNetWorthReport` from `calculateDashboardStats`, `buildMortgageReport` from `MortgageEngine.simulate(toMortgageInput(...))`, `buildPropertyReport` from `PropertyAsset` fields plus its attached mortgage, `buildCashFlowReport` from `calculateCashFlow`, `buildStrategyComparisonReport` from `projectPortfolio` + `createBaselineScenario` (the same two calls `ScenarioComparison` already makes). Net Worth Report is a snapshot breakdown of net worth *today*, not a historical trend — the app has no time-series storage, so "history" lives in the Dashboard's charts and Strategy Comparison instead, both projecting forward from now.
- **Export** (`src/domain/reporting/exportReport.ts`): `reportToCsv` (quote-escaped), `reportToJson`, and `downloadFile` — the app's first use of the Blob + `<a download>` browser API, a standard zero-dependency pattern rather than a new library. - **UI**: `ReportsHome` (`src/components/reporting/ReportsHome.tsx`) — six report-type cards; Portfolio Summary/Cash Flow/Net Worth go straight to the report (portfolio-wide), Mortgage/Property/Strategy Comparison show an entity picker if more than one exists, the report directly if there's exactly one, or a disabled card with a helper message if there are none (matching the zero-state pattern used everywhere else in the app). One generic `ReportView` renders all six, since they share `ReportData`.

## Mortgage detail: amortization view & extra payment advisor upgrade (done)

Requested directly by the user after Phase 7: `SplitCard`/`MortgagePanel` only ever showed summary stats, never the amortization schedule itself, and the extra payment advisor only tested a single one-off lump sum with no chart. Both were a presentation/engine-interface gap, not new simulation logic — the engine already computed full `AmortizationPayment[]` schedules and already supported recurring extra payments.

- **`combineSchedules`** (`src/domain/mortgage/amortizationDetail.ts`): every split in one mortgage shares the same frequency/start date, so schedules are index-aligned by `paymentNumber` and can be summed directly to get "the mortgage as a whole" — no re-simulation needed. **`annualAmortizationSummary`** derives each year's principal paid as `openingBalance − closingBalance` rather than summing `payment.principal` (which excludes extra payments and redistribution transfers), so it's correct regardless of *why* the balance moved that year.
- **`AmortizationDetail`** (`src/components/mortgage/AmortizationDetail.tsx`) — collapsed by default behind a "Show detail" toggle (SplitCard already has four other expandable sections). Expanded: two `TrendLineChart`s (Loan balance, Cumulative interest — reused unchanged from Phase 4/5) fed by the already-extracted `annualSeriesForSchedule`, an annual summary table, and a nested "Show full payment-by-payment schedule" toggle revealing every period in a scroll-capped table (360 rows for a 30-year monthly loan) rather than paginating. Mounted per split in `SplitCard` and once more in `MortgagePanel` for the combined schedule.
- **`recommendExtraPayment`** (`src/domain/mortgage/recommendExtraPayment.ts`) gained a `mode: "lump-sum" | "weekly" | "monthly"` parameter — `"lump-sum"` is the existing `BalanceAdjustmentEvent` path unchanged; `"weekly"`/`"monthly"` instead adds a `RecurringPayment` to the candidate split, a mechanism the engine already fully supported but the advisor never exercised. Each `ExtraPaymentOption` now also carries both schedules and payoff dates, so the UI can chart and report time-saved without re-simulating.
- **`ExtraPaymentAdvisor`** gained a payment-type selector (adornment changes `$ one-off` / `$/week` / `$/month`), a "Time saved" column, and two `MultiSeriesTrendChart` comparisons (Loan balance, Cumulative interest — baseline vs. with-extra-payment) for whichever split is selected in the table (defaults to the top-recommended one; clicking another row switches it). The paid-off-early series is padded out to the baseline's final year — zero for balance (visually flatlines at $0), held at its last value for interest (it doesn't reset once the loan's gone) — so "paid off years earlier" reads clearly on the chart instead of the line just stopping.

Verified: `tsc`/`eslint` clean. Engine-level (dynamic import): `combineSchedules`' totals matched `SummaryCard`'s own repayment/interest totals exactly; `annualAmortizationSummary`'s final-year closing balance was exactly 0 for a fully-amortising split, confirming it still lines up with the Phase 0 final-payment true-up; recurring mode correctly produces a *larger* total-life interest-saved figure than a same-nominal-amount lump sum (a $500/month forever payment easily out-contributes a single $500 lump sum — confirmed the ordering scales sensibly with the recurring amount, and that a fully-offset split still shows near-zero savings even at $500/month, same insight as the original lump-sum-only advisor). Browser: expanded a split's and the combined mortgage's amortization detail, confirmed both charts, the annual table, and the 360-row full schedule all rendered correctly and the final balance reached exactly $0; switched the advisor between lump-sum/weekly/monthly and confirmed the ranking, table, and both comparison charts updated correctly each time; clicked a non-recommended row and confirmed the charts switched to it. Console clean throughout.

## Extra payment advisor: life-of-loan reallocation & combined-summary (done)

Follow-up request: the advisor only ever answered "which single split, today?" — the user wanted the fuller question, "over the life of the loan, when should this money move — to another split once the current one's paid off or refixes to a different rate, or even to a savings account?" — plus a "what does the whole mortgage look like if I actually follow this?" summary. Confirmed with the user before design: reallocation triggers are a split being paid off *and* any split's scheduled refix (its future rate is known data, not predicted); a savings/cash account is a valid destination, compared using its own `interestRate`.

- **The one real engine change**: `RecurringPayment.endDate?: string` (`src/domain/mortgage/RecurringPayment.ts`), respected in `SimulationRunner.occurrencesInPeriod` (`src/engines/SimulationRunner.ts`). Needed because chaining regimes ("pay split A, then split B once A refixes") requires a prior regime's payment to actually *stop* at the boundary — a payoff-triggered handoff already self-limits (the engine caps extra payments at the remaining balance), but a refix-triggered one doesn't, so without an end date the old and new destination would both receive the payment in the test simulation. Verified byte-identical output when `endDate` is omitted — fully backward compatible.
- **`planExtraPaymentReallocation`** (`src/domain/mortgage/planExtraPaymentReallocation.ts`) chains a sequence of "regimes": at each step, every eligible split and interest-bearing cash account is evaluated over the *same bounded window* — up to the next scheduled refix anywhere in the mortgage (found by scanning already-computed schedules for a stored rate change, reusing engine output instead of duplicating `SimulationRunner`'s private refix-date math) — and the one creating the most value (interest saved for a split, interest earned for savings) wins. **A real design bug caught and fixed during engine-level testing**: the first version evaluated each candidate "permanently to the plan horizon," which let a savings account's uncapped decades of compounding unfairly out-score a split that would naturally pay off (and hand off to a next regime) long before the horizon — switching to a common bounded window fixed it, confirmed against a constructed scenario with an obvious right answer at each stage.
- **`compareCombinedMortgage`** (`src/domain/mortgage/combinedMortgageComparison.ts`): baseline vs. with-changes totals (interest, repaid, payoff date) for the *whole* mortgage via `combineSchedules`, reused for both the existing single-split recommendation and the new reallocation plan — one small function, not duplicated per caller.
- **UI**: `ExtraPaymentAdvisor` gained a "Combined position if you follow this" block (always on, cheap to compute) under the existing table; `ReallocationPlanPanel` (`src/components/mortgage/ReallocationPlanPanel.tsx`, mounted only for weekly/monthly mode — a reallocation plan doesn't apply to a lump sum) adds an explicit "Plan my reallocation" button (heavy chained computation, same pattern as Monte Carlo) producing a readable regime timeline, a savings-contribution table, the same combined-position comparison, and balance/interest charts reusing `MultiSeriesTrendChart` unchanged.

Verified: `tsc`/`eslint` clean. ## Reallocation planner fix: blind to rebalance-only refix triggers (done)

Diagnosed directly with the user from a real, surprising result: with an offset split ($100k value target) plus two $200k fixed splits (remainder-share 50/50, refixing every 24 months at the *same* rate — a realistic "rebalance the balance, don't predict a rate change" setup), the reallocation planner recommended sending every dollar straight to a 1.5% savings account instead of paying down either 5.75% fixed split. Root cause: `planExtraPaymentReallocation`'s "next trigger" detection (`findEarliestRateChange`) only watches for a *stored interest rate* changing between payments — it had no way to see a redistribution transfer that moves balance without changing rate, which is exactly what "rebalance at term completion, same rate" produces. With no trigger ever detected, the evaluation window fell back to the full 30-year plan horizon, so a fixed split that would actually pay itself off in ~8 years (confirmed by direct measurement) got no credit for the ~22 remaining years its freed-up capacity could still be doing something — while the savings option, uninterrupted by any of this, was credited for the full 30 years. Turning off `refixBalanceTarget` "fixed" the symptom only by coincidence (it removed a separate, legitimate effect — redistribution genuinely shrinking a fixed split's own marginal value — that happened to be large enough to flip the ranking back), not by closing the actual gap.

- **`findEarliestBalanceDiscontinuity`** (`src/domain/mortgage/planExtraPaymentReallocation.ts`): a general, cause-agnostic detector — scans for the next payment whose `openingBalance` doesn't match the previous payment's `closingBalance`, which is exactly what a redistribution transfer produces regardless of whether it also happens to change the rate. Combined with the existing rate-change detector into one `findNextTrigger`, replacing the single call site — no other change to the regime-chaining or window-bounding logic.

Verified: `tsc`/`eslint` clean. Re-ran the user's exact reproduction: regime boundaries now land on the fixed splits' real ~24-month rebalance cadence instead of the 30-year horizon, and the plan now recommends paying down Fixed A/B alternately for ~7.4 years before handing off to savings — the same *shape* the user got by disabling redistribution, now reached correctly with redistribution still on (total value created rose from $127,782 to $163,218, confirming the old answer was leaving real value on the table, not just "differently reasonable"). Regression-checked against the earlier verified scenarios from this conversation (obvious-answer 2-split-plus-savings case: identical regimes; single-floating-split-no-redistribution case: unchanged, since there's nothing for either detector to find there anyway). Browser: reproduced the exact scenario end-to-end in the running app and confirmed the corrected timeline renders; console clean throughout.

**Also fixed, same pass**: a separate, pre-existing, lower-severity issue surfaced during the regression testing above — a single-split mortgage with no alternative destination could show a spurious extra "regime" immediately after its own payoff. `scheduleAsOf.ts`'s `currentBalanceAsOf` answers "balance *before* today's payment," so at the exact instant a loan clears it still reports the last payment's (positive) opening balance rather than zero — correct for its other callers (e.g. the Dashboard's "as of today" stats), but wrong for this planner's use, where a regime boundary is always set to exactly a split's own payoff date. Added a local, stricter `isFullyPaidOff` (checks the *closing* balance of the last payment on-or-before the date) rather than changing the shared utility, since other call sites depend on its existing semantics. Confirmed the phantom regime is gone (`regimes.length` 2 → 1) and the dollar totals are byte-identical before and after — proving it was purely a cosmetic timeline glitch, never a financial error.

- **Follow-up bug found via the new chart, fixed in `projectPortfolio.ts`**: the user noticed the 2026 bar showing a wildly positive weekly cash flow ($13,800+) while the Dashboard's real "Weekly cash flow" stat showed a small negative figure. Root cause: each projection year's mortgage repayment is derived by diffing against *last* calendar year's balance/interest (`valueAtYear(mortgage.series, calendarYear - 1)`); for a mortgage whose real `startDate` falls within the *current* calendar year (no actual prior-year data exists), [`valueAtYear`](src/domain/mortgage/annualSeries.ts:30) correctly returns `0` for "before this loan existed" — but the code only guarded against this for a mortgage originated by a Buy-property strategy event (`isNewOrigination`, hardcoded `false` for every real, already-attached liability), not for an ordinary real mortgage that simply happens to be new. Using `0` as "last year's balance" instead of the original principal made that year's repayment swing hugely negative, which flipped the sign and inflated cash flow by roughly the loan's whole principal. Fixed by replacing the `isNewOrigination` flag-based check with a direct, data-driven one — `calendarYear - 1 < mortgage.series[0].year` — that catches both cases uniformly (a same-year real mortgage now correctly falls back to `originalPrincipal`, exactly like a same-year Buy event already did); the now-unused `isNewOrigination` field was removed from `MortgageProjection` and its three call sites. This is a correctness fix in the shared Strategy Simulator engine, not just the new Dashboard charts — any scenario projecting a mortgage that started within the current calendar year was affected.

- **Second follow-up bug, same root class**: after the mortgage fix, the user asked why year 2026 could still show *positive* cash flow against numbers ($700K loan, $950/week rent, $9K annual expenses) that clearly shouldn't be, from day one of ownership. Root cause: `projectPortfolio.ts` treats every projection year as "the full calendar year, Jan–Dec" uniformly — the mortgage side now correctly reflects only the real elapsed portion of a young loan's schedule, but property income/expenses had no equivalent concept at all. Every existing property gets `activeFromYear: 0` hardcoded ([projectPortfolio.ts:220](src/domain/scenario/projectPortfolio.ts:220)) with no reference to its actual `purchaseDate`, so a property bought two days ago was still credited a full 12 months of rent in year one — a full-year income credited against a partial-year mortgage cost, which is exactly what was flipping the sign. Buy-property-event properties get a constant `1`, since their construction already treats the buy as happening on Jan 1 of their own origination year — no double-proration.

- **Third follow-up, a display fix rather than a projection bug**: comparing the chart's "weekly" view against the Dashboard's own snapshot, the user asked why 2026 looked *less* negative than 2027 even though the underlying trend is a steady improvement from day one. Fixed by adding `weeksInYear` to `ProjectionYear` — 52 for a normal year, or (for offset 0 only) `round(52 × the smallest currentYearOwnershipFraction among that year's active properties)`, exact for a single-property portfolio and a reasonable, documented approximation for a mixed one. `calculateDashboardProjection.ts` surfaces this as `firstYearWeeks`, and `Dashboard.tsx`'s weekly conversion now divides the first point by `firstYearWeeks` and every later point by 52.

- **Fourth follow-up, found after Phase 15's vacancy work**: the user noticed the "annually" toggle still disagreed with the now-correct "weekly" one — 2026 looked *better* (less negative) than 2027 in annual mode, contradicting weekly mode's correctly-worse 2026.
